Do these plots help bidder 1 choose?

Yes. In every one of the nine cases the curve peaks at b = v. And because the peak is in the same place whatever the rival does, bidder 1 does not even need to know the rival's strategy: bidding the true value is always a best choice. That is a dominant strategy.

Why? Your bid only decides whether you win. The price is set by the rival. Bidding v means you win exactly when the price is below your value (profit) and lose exactly when it is above (avoiding a loss).

Flat regions. A half-value rival never bids above 0.5. So when v ≥ 0.5, every bid from 0.5 upward wins every auction and earns the same. Truthful bidding ties for best there (weakly dominant).

Two rivals (1C). You must beat the higher of two bids, so you win less often and earn less, but the peak is still at b = v.

The ruleHighest bid wins and the winner pays its own bid. Bidding your full value means zero profit.

Payments with truthful costs

The mechanism reproduces the textbook: path ABEF; AB paid 4, BE paid 2, EF paid 3, all others 0.

The buyer pays 9 for a path that truly costs 5. The extra 4 is the price of honesty: each chosen edge is paid what it saves the buyer, so its own report never changes its payment, only whether it is chosen.
